What the panel would build

  • Base: Sharp on Top. Brand, type, copy, the trader and the agency all start here: dark like the app, “They read one line. Make it hookworthy.”, each font with one job, a client switcher an agency would show a client.
  • From Show the Receipt: the static before→after (struck soft line, highlighted sharp line) readable without animation, and the receipt with move, kept and cut visible at rest in the first screen. Accessibility, mobile and positioning all chose it for this.
  • From Blue Pencil: marks drawn in reading order for the Sharpen moment, plus its skim headings (“Your facts stay. The fluff goes.”) and roman/italic heading couplets.
  • From Blue Field: the Studio mock with review link, client comment and Approve in one frame, and the feed of soft crypto openers as the “before” backdrop.
  • From Peel It Off: “Anyone with the link can comment. Only you can post.” and the peel-off stickers as merch or ad language, not the hero.

Fix before shipping, whichever wins

Show the receipt at rest in the first screen.

Move, kept and cut are the one thing competitors lack, and 4 of 5 designs hide them below the fold or behind an animation.

Raised by: Positioning

Land the Sharpen moment at 1.2–2 seconds.

Let the soft line be read first, then the edit. Peel It Off lands at about 4s; Show the Receipt finishes at about 1.25s; two never show it in the hero.

Raised by: Motion

Give every auto-cycling demo a pause.

Three designs fail WCAG 2.2.2, and Blue Pencil re-announces its text to screen readers every 7 seconds.

Raised by: Accessibility

Add a bottom “Sharpen my first post” bar on phones.

Every page runs 14–17 phone screens with 10–14 screens where the only signup is a 36–42px pill in the corner.

Raised by: Mobile

Reword replies so they can’t read as auto-replies.

“Replies come in already answered” breaks the never-auto-reply promise. Use “Replies come in with your answer drafted. You send it.”

Raised by: Copywriter

Keep lowercase voices lowercase, and let “Try yours” really rewrite.

Four of five rewrites capitalise a lowercase voice, and the try box only underlines soft words.

Raised by: Trader

Give Studio a worked quote and say who can post.

For example, 8 client voices = $144/mo billed yearly. Say how client accounts connect and who can press send.

Raised by: Agency

Shrink phone section headings to about 32px.

Headlines at 41–43px barely outrank 37–39px section titles on a phone.

Raised by: Type

Keep the amber squiggle as the brand mark.

It is the only mark the app already draws; stickers, lime and highlighter replace it on screen.

Copywriter8.4
Would ship this one

The headline names the exact unit of value and the small print has the driest, most specific writing on the panel. Fix one overpromise and it ships.

Best: "They read one line. Make it hookworthy." states the insight, then turns the brand name into the payoff. The receipt line "$10k stops the thumb. 3 years makes it believable." is the best single sentence across all five pages. The trust rows "Auto-DMs: Not a setting. Not ever." and "Auto-reposts: No surprise reruns." are dry and exact, and "Sharpening is free. Forever." is the most persuasive and most honest pricing heading in the set. "Sharpen my first line" is the most precise primary CTA: the product works on lines, not posts.

Problems
  • The closing line overpromises: "Good ideas die in bad first lines. Yours won't." It guarantees an outcome on a page that elsewhere refuses to promise reach.
  • In the features section the biggest type is a list of module names ("Previews / Week / Inbox / Results / Review links"), which is SaaS filler. The real benefit lines are small, and "Replies come with answers." and "Your week, best times marked." read flatter than "already circled".
  • The 11 PM lede, "...and it goes out at 8:40 AM when your people scroll", sounds like autopilot. Say that you schedule it.
  • "Try it here" is a placeholder secondary CTA. Studio repeats itself: "6 clients, 6 voices, 1 inbox." sits right next to the H2 "Six clients. Six voices."

Fix: Change the closing line "Yours won't." to "Yours don't have to. Sharpen one now, free." and change the secondary CTA "Try it here" to "See the receipts".

Accessibility auditor6.3

Thoughtful engineering on motion and good receipt semantics, let down by a phone picker with no names and white clock numerals on a sunrise photo.

Best: The motion is handled with care: the 6.5 s cycle pauses on hover and on focusin, stops when off-screen, and is disabled under reduced motion. The hero has no live region, so it never spams screen readers. The sharpened post is a labelled <article> and the receipt is a Kept/Cut <dl>.

Problems
  • SC 4.1.2 (axe critical): at 390 the four #pick buttons in the hero are avatar-only with no accessible name. A screen reader hears 'button, button, button, button'.
  • SC 1.4.3: '8:40 AM' in white over the sunrise photo samples at 2.4:1 (large text needs 3:1), and the 'AM'/'PM' suffix in #D5D8DF is 1.6:1.
  • SC 2.2.2: there's no visible pause, and the pause on hover/focus doesn't help screen-reader browse mode (no focus event), so the hero swaps content under the virtual cursor every 6.5 s. Clicking a chip restarts the timer instead of stopping it.
  • The playground textarea relies on an inset rgba(117,162,255,.55) glow at about 3:1 (marginal under 1.4.11). Div aria-labels on .stage, .nets, .wk and .locks are ignored. The Studio client list clips at 320px.

Fix: Give the phone chips aria-labels ('$10k month', 'Sizing down', …) and stop the rotation for good once the user picks a chip or presses Sharpen, plus a small visible Pause button.

Best: It uses Inter's real optical-size axis (opsz 32, verified in the font) at 680 weight and 130px with 0.93 leading. 'Write the line / they stop for.' is tightly built and fills the phone width at 56px without breaking the 2-line rule.

Problems
  • 99% of the text is Inter. Soft vs sharp and before vs after are carried only by colour and strikethrough, never by type, on a page about writing.
  • Nine H2s in identical 85px bold with no tonal or italic contrast, so every section shouts at the same volume. 'Premium / 500 / 500K' in display bold competes with them.
  • The -0.052em tracking is applied unchanged at 56px on phone, and opsz 32 display spacing is forced onto 18 to 22px H3s and pricing names.
  • The desktop hero lede is about 24 characters per line. The decorative feed sets thousands of characters at 13.5px behind the hero card, and the struck old line is cut off with 'af…'.

Fix: Bring a second voice back: set the sharpened post line and the second line of each H2 in Newsreader (or at least a lighter weight). Loosen tracking as size drops (about -0.03em at 44 to 56px) and let opsz run automatically on the small headings.

Mobile + performance6.3

Engineered like a mobile page should be, lightest by 3x and shortest by 2-3 screens, but it drops its CTA after the hero and the headline jumps under your thumb.

Best: About 302KB total (about 262KB gzipped): two fonts with Inter preloaded, no photo sprite, a text LCP at 0.63s, and fonts done by 1.6s while the others finish at 2.5-3.8s. Pricing arrives at screen 10, against 12.7-13.6 everywhere else.

Problems
  • CLS 0.103 from a single shift at about 1.4s, when the Inter swap re-wraps the display h1. The headline moves 53px and the subhead plus CTAs move about 104px, right when a thumb is heading for Start free. With fonts blocked, CLS is 0.
  • The header is position:absolute, not sticky. After the hero there is no signup control on screen for 10.5 screens, until pricing at about 8,500px.
  • The 'before' line is truncated to one line with an ellipsis and struck through at about 13px, so the transformation is not readable on a phone. The hero shows a result rather than the tap.
  • The DOM is the largest in the set at 1,182 nodes: the drift feed is 8 columns of duplicated cards with 8 infinite animations behind the hero. TBT is 344ms, and recurring 55-64ms tasks were still firing at about 10s idle.

Fix: Add a metric-matched fallback (@font-face local('Arial') with size-adjust/ascent-override tuned to Inter Display) so the h1 cannot reflow, and make the header sticky with Start free. Two small changes that would make this the strongest mobile page here.

Positioning strategist6.3

Sells the outcome in the feed so well that it reads like every 'write viral posts' tool. The receipt, the actual moat, is missing from the hero.

Best: The drifting feed of soft openers ('hot take but i kind of think', 'not to brag but', from handles like onchain otto and deltaneutral) names the enemy at a glance. It's the most crypto-native framing in the set.

Problems
  • 'Write the line they stop for' is an outcome promise in growth-tool grammar (stop-the-scroll is Tweet Hunter, Postwise and Hypefury territory), and Inter on a big blue field looks like Taplio or Tweet Hunter. The category of one becomes 'AI writer for X'.
  • No wedge above the fold: no receipt and no 'your facts stay'. The hero card shows invented body copy ('I lost $4,000 in March…') the engine never wrote, so it's selling a whole post, not a first line.
  • 'Posts get paid.' overstates the change (only accounts past Premium/500/500K get paid), and the giant Premium/500/500K tiles look like Hookworthy's own stats at a glance, which is exactly the invented-proof look a skeptic is scanning for.
  • 'Try a line' opens four presets you can't type into, and Pro/Studio show 'Billed yearly' without the $16/$24 monthly prices until you toggle. Small credibility leaks on a page with no other proof.

Fix: Swap the invented body copy on the hero post for its receipt ('Made it a question · Kept: sizing down, after a loss · 17→10 words') so the mechanism, not the outcome, is what you see first.

Copywriter6.2

Keynote fragments dropped into a creator's feed: tidy, forgettable, and the brand's best line is saved for the end.

Best: The drifting feed of real-sounding soft openers ("hot take but i kind of think", "not to brag but i think i finally figured something out") shows the problem better than any headline. "It's not a strategy. It's a seatbelt." in the example thread is the best line on the page, and "Try a line" is a good, tiny CTA.

Problems
  • "Write the line they stop for." is generic (it fits any copywriting tool) and leans toward a reach promise ("they stop"). The punchline "Good ideas die in bad first lines." is buried in the closing H2.
  • The headings are telegraphic ("Soft in. Sharp out." "One draft. Every feed." "Any niche. Same fix.") and need the visuals to mean anything. "Every feed" overclaims, since it supports four. "Any niche" contradicts "Traders first".
  • Feature labels are flat or misleading: "Idea caught", "Replies, answered", "Posted when they scroll". The passive voice suggests auto-reply and auto-post.
  • The examples are sloppy. "Nora Quill, ghostwriter for 3 clients" is paired with the trader's "$10k month trading" line. "Posts get paid. Replies don't." drops X as the payer. "Choose Pro / Choose Studio" is SaaS boilerplate.

Fix: Use "Good ideas die in bad first lines." as the H1 over the feed, and keep "Write the line they stop for." for the Sharpen section.

Accessibility auditor5.4

The hero text and white focus rings on the blue are solid, but the drifting feed is an endless motion loop with no pause and trips over 100 automated contrast failures.

Best: The blue was deepened so white text and the white focus ring hold about 6:1. The hero post is a labelled <article> with an AT-visible 'Was:' prefix, the decorative feed is aria-hidden, and the try-a-line demo works by keyboard with focus retained.

Problems
  • SC 2.2.2 fail: 8 feed columns run infinite CSS drift beside the H1 with no pause control. Reduced-motion users are spared; everyone else with vestibular or attention issues is not.
  • SC 1.4.3: axe reports 107 serious contrast violations at 1440 and 117 at 390. All are aria-hidden feed posts (white at 50–70% alpha on blue, 2.5–3.9:1). They are real readable sentences, not 'pure decoration', so every procurement scan will flag the page.
  • SC 1.3.1 landmarks: <header> and the main <nav> sit inside <main> (inside the hero region), so there's no banner landmark and 'main' starts with site navigation.
  • SC 2.4.3: the Sharpen button comes after the four example picks in tab order although it sits visually between them. The hero card truncates the 'Was:' line with an ellipsis, so sighted and zoom users never see the full before.

Fix: Freeze the feed by default or add a Pause control, and turn the decoy posts into abstract bars or blurred shapes (no legible words), which removes both the 2.2.2 and the 1.4.3 findings in one change.

Motion designer4.8

Beautiful wallpaper: the feed drifts and the one sharp post just sits there, so the product's signature move never happens above the fold.

Best: The Try section's swap blurs the old result out (blur 8px, translateY 8px, .45s) and focus-pulls the new one in. That's the right literal metaphor for 'sharpen', and the restrained .97 press on the button matches it.

Problems
  • The first 3 seconds are static except a background drift of about 11px/s (64–92s loops). It reads as ambience and doesn't point the eye anywhere.
  • No soft-to-sharp moment in the hero. The 'was' line is struck in tiny grey type and the post is already labelled 'Sharpened'. It's a still diagram of an edit, not the edit.
  • During the Try swap the new 'before' appears instantly while the old 'after' is still blurring out, so for a beat they're mismatched (Dev Raman's draft over heycape's result).
  • Hovers are colour-only. The scroll reveals are generic fade-ups, and the drift runs forever with no pause when off-screen.

Fix: Bring the blur-to-focus move into the hero. The white card arrives as the soft draft (amber underlines, slight blur) and at about 0.8s focus-pulls into the sharp post while the soft feed keeps drifting behind it.

Brand strategist4.3

Clean and confident, and almost nothing in it is ownable. Inter on a blue field with white post cards is how the networks themselves look.

Best: 'Write the line they stop for.' with one crisp post standing still in a drifting, blurred feed, its old line struck through above the sharp one. It is the clearest single picture of the outcome.

Problems
  • A blue field with white post cards and an Inter headline is the visual language of Bluesky, LinkedIn and old Twitter. In a feed this ad blends into the platform's own UI instead of stopping the thumb.
  • It drops Newsreader, the only thing that set the current brand apart from sans-serif SaaS. What's left (an 'h' tile, Inter, white cards) is unownable.
  • The drifting feed is a low-contrast metaphor. In the first half-second it's blurred blue noise, and the idea only lands once you read the post.
  • There is no brand device that repeats down the page besides the blue, so the sections could be shuffled into any startup's site.

Fix: Keep 'one still post in a moving feed' as a motion-ad format, but move it into the current brand: a dark feed, the post set in Newsreader, and amber squiggles on the soft ghost posts so the sharp one is the only clean line.
